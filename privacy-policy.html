Privacy Policy for Alarmy

Last updated 24 October 2026

Alarmy has no accounts and no servers of its own. Your alarms, tones, photos and wake-up history stay on your device. The one thing that leaves it is anonymous crash reports and usage statistics, sent through Google Firebase so bugs can be found and fixed — and you can switch that off in Settings. Messages from us and the AI assistant are separate switches, and both are off unless you turn them on.

What the app stores, and where

All of the following is written only to Alarmy's own private storage area on your device. None of it is transmitted anywhere, and it is not readable by other apps.

  • Your alarms — times, labels, repeat days, alarm type and quiz settings — in a local database file.
  • Your preferences — the chosen theme, wallpaper and alarm tone — in the platform's standard local settings store.
  • Tones and photos you import. When you pick an audio file or a photo, Alarmy copies it into its own storage and uses that copy. It does not keep a link back into your library, and it does not read anything you did not explicitly pick.
  • Edited copies. Cropping a photo or trimming a sound writes a second file next to the original, and keeps the original so the edit can be changed later. Deleting the tone or wallpaper removes both.
  • Your wake-up history — when each alarm rang and whether you dismissed it, snoozed it or missed it — in the same local database, so the app can show you how you have been getting up. It is kept for about four months and is not sent anywhere, except as the summary counts described under “AI assistant”, if you turn that on and ask about it.

Crash reports and usage statistics

Alarmy uses two Google services, Firebase Crashlytics and Google Analytics for Firebase, to learn when the app crashes and which features are used. Together with the optional “Tips and news” and “AI assistant” described below, this is the app’s only use of the internet. Nothing an alarm needs depends on it: with no connection the app works exactly the same, and reports simply wait.

You can switch it off at any time in Settings, under “Share crash reports and usage”. Nothing further is collected once it is off.

  • Crash reports — what went wrong and where in the code, the app version, your device model and operating system version, and when it happened.
  • Usage statistics — counts of things like opening the app, saving an alarm and which type it was, plus general information such as your device model, operating system, language and country.
  • These are linked to a random identifier created for this installation, not to your name, an account or an advertising identifier. Advertising identifiers are switched off.
  • What you create is never sent: no alarm labels or times, no tones, no photos and no wake-up history.
  • Google processes this data on our behalf under its own privacy policy, at policies.google.com/privacy.

Tips and news

The Reminders screen has a “Tips and news” switch. It is off unless you turn it on. While it is on, Alarmy can receive occasional messages from us through Firebase Cloud Messaging, a Google service.

  • Turning it on gives your device a push token, a random identifier that lets a message reach it. The token says nothing about who you are.
  • Turning it off deletes the token, and no token exists if you never turned it on.
  • Everyone who has it on gets the same messages. Nothing about your alarms or how you use the app is used to choose them.
  • The other reminders on that screen are separate: they are worked out on your device and use no internet at all.

AI assistant

The Ask button on the Alarms screen opens an optional AI assistant. It is off unless you turn it on, and it asks first, explaining what is sent. It is answered by Google Gemini through Firebase AI Logic, a Google service, so it needs the internet; nothing else in the app does.

  • What you type in the chat is sent to Google to be answered.
  • The microphone button types what you say into the message box. Your phone’s own speech service turns it into text — Google’s on Android, Apple’s on iPhone, on the iPhone itself where your language allows. Alarmy receives only the text and keeps no recording, and nothing reaches the assistant until you press Send.
  • Your alarms — labels, times, repeat days, whether each is on, and its type — are sent only when a question needs them, as the assistant answers it.
  • Your wake-up history is sent only as summary counts — how many wake-ups were on time, snoozed or missed, by weekday — and only when a question needs it. Individual wake-ups are never sent.
  • Never sent: photos, sounds, or anything at all while the assistant is off.
  • When you ask it to, the assistant sets, changes, switches off or deletes alarms on your device. Each change shows Undo, and the change itself is made on your device, not by Google.
  • While the assistant is in beta it uses Google’s free service, under which Google may use these chats to improve its products. The chat itself is kept only on your device and is cleared when you turn the assistant off.

Reminders

The Reminders screen offers five optional notifications: a bedtime reminder, a warning when no alarm is set for tomorrow, a weekly summary, your streak, and a tip when you have been snoozing a lot. Each has its own switch and all of them are off until you turn them on.

  • They are worked out on your device, from your own alarms and wake-up history, and scheduled by the device itself.
  • They use no internet. Nothing about them, and nothing they are calculated from, is sent to us or to anyone else.
  • Turning a reminder off cancels it. Which ones you have on is stored only in the app’s settings on your device.

Stopping collection and deleting data

Alarmy has no accounts, so the data described above cannot be tied to you by name, and there is no profile for us to look up.

  • To stop crash reports and usage statistics, turn off “Share crash reports and usage” in Settings. It takes effect at once.
  • To stop messages from us, turn off “Tips and news”. That deletes the push token.
  • To stop the AI assistant, turn off “AI assistant” in Settings. Nothing more is sent, and the chat is cleared.
  • To remove everything on your device, uninstall the app. That deletes your alarms, tones, photos, wake-up history and settings, and the installation’s random identifier with them.
  • Reports already sent are kept by Google for a limited time — crash reports for 90 days — and then deleted. Because they carry no name or account, we cannot pick out one person’s reports to delete them sooner.

Sending feedback

Settings has a “Send feedback” option. It does not send anything itself: it opens a draft in whatever email app you already use, with the message you typed, and nothing leaves your device unless you tap send there.

  • The draft is pre-filled with the app version, your platform and OS version, and which alarm permissions are missing — so a report about an alarm not ringing can be acted on.
  • You can read and edit all of it before sending, and delete the draft instead if you would rather not.
  • No alarms, tones, photos or identifiers are included.

Keeping the app up to date

On Android, Alarmy asks the Play Store app already on your phone whether a newer version exists. That is a message between two apps on the same device — Alarmy opens no connection, and the Play Store, which checks for updates anyway, is the one that uses the network.

If an update is marked as required, Google Play shows its own update screen and installs it. Alarmy sends nothing and receives nothing about you.

Settings also has a “Check for update” option, which makes the same local ask and either tells you the app is up to date or opens your app’s page in the Play Store. Nothing about you is sent either way.

There is no equivalent on iPhone. Apple provides no way for an app to ask what the latest version is, so updates there are handled by the App Store itself and the option is not shown.

Rating the app

Settings has a “Rate app” option, and a one-time prompt appears after your first alarm. Neither sends us anything.

With a connection it asks the store — Google Play, or the App Store on iPhone — to show its own rating sheet; without one it opens the store listing instead. That is your device’s store app doing its own work. Alarmy passes it nothing about you and is told nothing back, not even whether a sheet appeared.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, or personal details of any kind.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No data sold or shared with anyone for advertising or marketing. The only third-party code is Google Firebase, including the AI assistant, described above, and Google Play’s own rating sheet and update check, each handed a request and nothing about you.
  • No location, contacts, calendar, microphone, camera or health access.
  • No alarm, tone, photo or wake-up history leaves your device, except what the AI assistant is sent to answer a question, if you turn it on.

Permissions, and why each one exists

Alarmy asks only for what an alarm clock needs in order to actually wake you, plus the internet access described above. The alarm permissions are not used to gather information.

  • Alarms & reminders / exact alarms — to ring at the exact minute you set rather than whenever the system finds convenient.
  • Notifications — the ringing alarm is a notification; without it the alarm screen cannot open. The optional reminders in Settings are notifications too. They are worked out on your device and nothing about them is sent anywhere.
  • Full-screen notifications (Android) — so the alarm appears over the lock screen, the way the built-in clock does.
  • Run at startup (Android) — alarms are not kept by the system across a restart, so the app re-registers them after a reboot.
  • Foreground service, wake lock and vibration (Android) — to keep the tone playing and the screen awake while the alarm rings.
  • Ignore battery optimisations (Android, optional) — battery saving can delay an alarm; this keeps an overnight alarm on time.
  • Network state (Android) — reading whether the device currently has a connection, so “Rate app” knows whether to ask for the store’s rating sheet or open the listing instead.
  • Internet — used only to send the crash reports and usage statistics described above, and to receive “Tips and news” if you turned it on. Nothing an alarm needs uses it.

Choosing a tone or a photo

Picking a tone or a photo uses the system's own file and photo pickers. Those hand the app one chosen file and grant no access to the rest of your library, which is why Alarmy never asks for photo or storage permission.

Editing happens on your device. Cropping a photo and trimming a sound are done by the app itself, on the file already in its storage. Nothing is uploaded and no editing service is involved.

On Android, the list of alarm sounds is the one your phone already has. Alarmy reads their names to show them and plays them where they are; it copies nothing and changes nothing. iOS provides no way to do this, so there the list is the set built into the app.

Deleting a tone or wallpaper in the app deletes the copied file. Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

Children

Alarmy is suitable for all ages and is not directed at children. The anonymous crash reports and usage statistics described above are the only data it sends, and they can be switched off in Settings. “Tips and news” is off unless someone turns it on.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app ever changes what it stores or what it sends, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
